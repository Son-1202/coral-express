# T-25 handoff (DEV): smooth train motion

## Status
Done. `npm run build` passes. `npm run test` passes: 29 files, 179 tests (2 new files).

## Diagnosis (measured with `scripts/motion-probe.ts`, virtual 60 fps rAF clock, mid-leg cruise)
1. **Root cause: 4 Hz stepping.** `GameController.pose()` read `sim.state.train.t`, which only changes on each 0.25 s sim tick. At 60 fps, the train's world X stayed still for 280 of 300 frames, then jumped about 0.47 world units (about 38 px) every 4th frame. The camera eased every frame, so the train jumped forwards and backwards against the screen. That is the producer's strip.
2. **Camera also stepped.** The look-ahead lead (`speed·0.45`) was built from the stepped velocity. The follow `x += (T−x)(1−e^{−k·dt})` also has a lag that depends on dt: with 16/33 ms frames it alternates by about 1.3 px.
3. **Slosh / rail-joint rocking.** Slosh is driven by `Δvel/dt`, so it spiked on every tick. The joint bump phase used `time·speed`, which is not integrated, so it ran at very high frequency during speed ramps. Wheels turned by `speed·dt` on the clamped dt.
4. **Not causes.** Parallax layers and stations are positioned from the camera X, so they were smooth once the camera was. There is no pixel snapping on the camera or sprites.

## Fix
- `src/game/pose.ts`: `trainPose(..., ahead)` adds the sim's unconsumed sub-tick time (`state.tickAcc`). The pose is then a continuous function of time that equals the sim exactly at every tick. A leg end or stop end that falls inside the sub-tick is resolved the way the next tick will resolve it, so there is no overshoot. `controller.pose()` passes `tickAcc`.
- `src/render/diorama/cameraRig.ts`: new `followStep()`. It integrates the exponential follow exactly for a moving target, so the lag is independent of frame time.
- `src/render/stage.ts`: the camera follow and the train accel use the real frame gap, capped at 0.25 s, because the train follows real sim time.
- `src/render/train/train.ts`: wheels turn by the actual Δx. Rail-joint rocking is driven by distance rolled.
